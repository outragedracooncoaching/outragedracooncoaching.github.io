Title: A lot of women are quietly planning their exit from tech (Titlecased Claude pls)
Date: 2 months ago

A lot of women are quietly planning their exit from tech.

Not mildly tired.
Not “maybe I need a holiday” tired.
Done.

👉 Bakery, personal trainer, freelance, public sector, small business, “literally anything but this” levels of done. 

I know women who have already left tech to do exactly those things.
I’m seeing it in coaching chats, coffee chats and almost everywhere I go.

It’s especially showing up with senior women over 40.
Women who have spent years building an Olympic-level tolerance for bullsh*t and have still reached the end of the line.

But it’s not just me.

💡 According to GOV.UK’s 2025 Diversity in UK Tech report, one in three women in tech are planning to leave their roles, citing lack of career progression, poor work-life balance and unsupportive culture.
💡 Akamai’s 2026 Mid-Career Exodus research found that 87% of women leave tech roles within ten years.

And this isn’t just a “diversity is a nice-to-have” problem.

💡 McKinsey found that companies in the top quartile for gender diversity on executive teams were 39% more likely to financially outperform those in the bottom quartile.
💡 The 2025 Lovelace Report estimated that 40,000–60,000 women leave UK tech and digital roles each year, costing the economy £2bn–£3.5bn annually.

When experienced women leave tech, we don’t just lose representation.
We take a serious hit to competitive advantage, culture and innovation.

The data already tells us why.
The question is whether we’re finally willing to fix the conditions driving women out.
